## First, let's talk about the distances

There's something funny about planning a trip on Google Maps.

Everything looks so simple.

You see Marrakech, then the mountains, then the desert, then Fes. A few lines connecting the destinations, some driving hours, and suddenly the entire trip looks perfectly organized.

Until you actually take that road.

The drive from Marrakech to Merzouga, for example, takes you across the High Atlas Mountains and through some of the most beautiful landscapes in Morocco.

You'll see small villages, traditional kasbahs, valleys, and places where you might want to stop every twenty minutes to take a photograph.

And believe me, there are plenty of those places.

But the roads are winding, the distances are significant, and stopping to explore takes time.

A journey that looks manageable on a map can become a very long day.

That's why I usually prefer dividing the journey into smaller sections.

You can actually get out of the car, explore somewhere, have lunch without rushing, and enjoy the road.

Because sometimes the road itself is one of the best parts of traveling in Morocco.

## So, what would I recommend for seven days?

If you're visiting Morocco for the first time, one of my favorite routes to recommend is Marrakech to Fes through the Sahara Desert.

It gives you a beautiful introduction to the country.

You experience the energy of Marrakech, cross the Atlas Mountains, discover the traditional villages of southern Morocco, spend a night in the desert, and finish in the historic city of Fes.

And each place feels completely different.

Here's how I'd plan it.

### Day 1: Welcome to Marrakech

Arrive, check into your riad, and take some time to settle in.

If you're feeling energetic, explore the area around Jemaa el-Fnaa or enjoy dinner somewhere in the medina.

I wouldn't schedule a full afternoon of activities after an international flight.

### Day 2: A full day in Marrakech

Explore the historic medina with a licensed local guide. Visit places such as Bahia Palace and the Ben Youssef Madrasa, then spend time discovering the souks.

And leave a little free time.

Seriously.

You don't need someone telling you where to go every minute of your holiday.

### Day 3: Marrakech to Dades Valley

This is where the road trip really begins.

Cross the High Atlas Mountains through the Tizi n'Tichka Pass, visit Aït Ben Haddou, and continue through Ouarzazate toward Dades.

You'll probably stop several times for photographs.

That's normal.

The landscapes are worth it.

Spend the night in the Dades Valley.

### Day 4: Dades Valley to Merzouga

After breakfast, continue toward Todra Gorge and eventually the Sahara.

By the afternoon, you'll reach Merzouga, where the dunes of Erg Chebbi begin.

Depending on your arrival time and preferences, you can experience a sunset camel ride or head directly to your desert camp.

Spend the night surrounded by the dunes.

### Day 5: Merzouga to Fes

I'll be clear about this day.

It's long.

The drive usually takes around eight to ten hours with stops, sometimes longer depending on road conditions.

You'll travel through the Ziz Valley, Midelt, and the Middle Atlas before reaching Fes.

For some travelers, that's absolutely fine.

For others, especially families with young children, I'd recommend adding an overnight stop in Midelt and adjusting the itinerary.

### Day 6: Discovering Fes

Take a guided tour through the old medina, explore traditional craftsmanship, visit historical monuments, and discover the atmosphere of the city.

Fes deserves time.

After the long journey from the desert, having a full day here makes a difference.

### Day 7: Departure

Enjoy your last breakfast and transfer to the airport.

Ideally, you'd fly into Marrakech and depart from Fes. If your flights require returning to Marrakech or Casablanca, the route needs to be reconsidered.

And there you have it.

Seven days.

Three very different sides of Morocco.

Of course, this is one possible itinerary. Your arrival city, departure airport, interests, and tolerance for driving should determine the final route.

## There's one thing about the Sahara I want to mention

The Sahara Desert is probably one of the most requested experiences we receive.

And I understand why.

The dunes are beautiful, especially around sunset.

But something I always try to explain is that getting to Merzouga takes time.

Sometimes people want to leave Marrakech in the morning, reach the desert, enjoy a camel ride, spend the night, and return the following day.

That's a lot of driving for one night.

And while a short desert excursion is possible, I think travelers should understand what they're booking.

If you have enough time, spending two nights around Merzouga gives you a completely different experience.

You can visit nearby villages, listen to Gnawa music in Khamlia, explore the desert surroundings by 4x4, or spend a quiet afternoon doing absolutely nothing.

And doing nothing in the desert?

That's actually a pretty good activity. Haha.

For a seven-day Marrakech-to-Fes itinerary, though, a second desert night means making a trade-off somewhere else.

That's the reality of planning a trip.

Every extra activity or destination costs time.

## The most expensive tour isn't always the best

Something else I've noticed in tourism is how much attention people give to hotel categories.

Four-star hotels. Five-star hotels. Luxury camps. Premium vehicles.

And yes, comfort is important.

But I think there are other things worth paying attention to.

How experienced is your driver?

Does the itinerary allow enough time to enjoy each destination?

Are the accommodations actually located where you want to be?

Does the company explain what is included in the price?

And what happens if something goes wrong?

These are questions I'd ask before booking any tour.

A beautiful hotel won't make a ten-hour drive disappear.

And a luxury desert camp won't help much if you arrive too late to enjoy the sunset you were looking forward to.

Good planning matters.

Sometimes more than people realize.

## Maybe we're all trying to see too much

I understand why travelers try to fit everything into one trip.

Flights are expensive. Holidays are limited. And when you're traveling somewhere new, there's always that feeling that you might miss something important.

But Morocco isn't going anywhere.

Chefchaouen will still be beautiful on your next visit.

Essaouira will still have its ocean breeze.

And Marrakech will probably still be as busy as ever. Haha.

You can always come back.

I'd rather see someone enjoying a relaxed breakfast in a riad than rushing out at 6 AM every morning because there are another four destinations to check off.

And that's something I try to keep in mind when we design private tours at Morocco Travel Experts.

A good itinerary should respect your time.

Your interests.

And your energy.

> *Seven days are enough to experience something beautiful in Morocco. They're not enough to experience everything, and that's perfectly okay.*

## One last thought before you book

If you're comparing different Morocco itineraries, don't look only at how many places each tour includes.

Look at how much time you'll actually spend enjoying them.

A seven-day trip with three or four meaningful destinations can be more enjoyable than one with eight stops and barely enough time to unpack your suitcase.

Ask questions before paying a deposit.

Check the driving distances.

Make sure the accommodation, activities, and services are clearly explained.

And tell your tour operator what matters most to you.

Maybe it's the Sahara.

Maybe it's Moroccan food, architecture, photography, or spending time with local people.

Maybe you simply want a peaceful week away from your daily routine.

Whatever it is, build your journey around that.

Because the **best 7 day Morocco tour** is the one that fits the experience you're hoping to have.

And sometimes, choosing where not to go is the most important decision you'll make.
